About Me

I'm a sophomore at the University of Florida pursuing a B.S. in Microbiology and Cell Science with a minor in Early Childhood Studies. My academic journey combines rigorous scientific training with hands-on clinical experience, and I'm working toward a career in pediatric medicine.

Through my role as President of UF Dream Team, I lead a hospital-based volunteer organization in the Pediatric ICU and Pediatric Cardiac ICU at UF Health Shands Children's Hospital. This experience has been the most fulfilling part of my college career and solidified my commitment to working with children in healthcare settings.

Additionally, I'm involved in organic chemistry research at the Feng Lab, where I work on improving aryne generation methods, and I serve as Music Production Lead for the UF Audio Engineering Society, teaching music production to 30+ students using Ableton Live 12.

Reflections & Journal

Leadership and Growth Through Dream Team

Spring 2026

Taking on the role of President for Dream Team has been both challenging and incredibly rewarding. Managing application cycles with around 500 applicants each semester requires a lot of organization, but more importantly, it's taught me about creating meaningful experiences for volunteers and patients alike.

The work we do in the Pediatric ICU and Pediatric Cardiac ICU goes beyond just providing comfort to kids. It's about being present during some of the most difficult moments families face, and helping create small moments of normalcy and joy. This experience has confirmed my desire to pursue pediatric medicine and has shown me how much clinical work connects with child development principles from my minor.

Balancing Research and Clinical Work

Fall 2025

Working in the Feng Lab has given me a new appreciation for how methodical and detail-oriented research needs to be. Every reaction setup, every base we test, every measurement matters when you're trying to improve synthetic methods. It's a very different pace from clinical volunteering, but I'm learning how both research and patient care require precision, patience, and adaptability.

I'm starting to see how these experiences will complement each other in medical school and beyond. Research teaches you to ask better questions and think critically about evidence, while clinical work reminds you why that evidence matters in the first place.

Music Production as Creative Balance

Fall 2025

Leading the Audio Engineering Society's music production program has been an unexpected highlight of this year. Teaching 30+ students how to use Ableton Live is completely different from anything else I do academically or clinically, and that's exactly why it's so valuable. Music production is pure creativity and problem-solving without the pressure of grades or clinical stakes.

It's also taught me a lot about teaching itself. Breaking down complex software into digestible lessons and helping students find their own creative voice requires patience and adaptability. These are skills I know will transfer to patient education and working with families in clinical settings.
